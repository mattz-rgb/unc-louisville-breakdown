The result

Louisville beat North Carolina 16-14 at Kenan Stadium. My pick of UNC 34-31 missed the winner and missed the shape of the game by a wide margin.

I expected a shootout and got 30 combined points. The Tar Heels fell to 5-5 (2-4 ACC) and still need a sixth win to become bowl-eligible. Louisville moved to 7-3 (6-2) with its fifth straight win, scoring 27.5 fewer points than it had averaged over the previous four games.

The box score looks fair. UNC averaged more yards a play, won third down and threw for more yards. Four things decided it that the table only half shows. UNC allowed six sacks on 31 dropbacks, about one in five. It lost the turnover battle 2-0, and the one that hurt was Billy Edwards Jr.'s second-quarter fumble, which Louisville recovered at the UNC 21 and turned into the only touchdown its offense scored, a 4-play, 21-yard drive finished by a 3-yard Lincoln Kienholz pass to Jaleel Skinner. UNC lost its starting quarterback midway through the third quarter. And it went 0-for-2 on fourth down, and the second miss was the last play of the game.

02

Game flow

Louisville led from the middle of the second quarter on, but UNC was never out of it. UNC opened with a 12-play, 75-yard drive that Benjamin Hall finished with an 11-yard run and led 7-3. Louisville then scored 10 unanswered points in the second quarter, and it went to the half up 13-7 while outgaining UNC 214 yards to 89. The Tar Heels managed 14 yards on everything after the opening drive in the first half.

03

Offense by position group

UNC ran 59 offensive snaps. The numbers check out: the quarterbacks, backs, linemen, tight ends and receivers add up to exactly 11 players a snap, so nothing's missing. Shares are of those 59 snaps.

Quarterback

Billy Edwards Jr. took 32 snaps (54 percent) and Travis Burgess 27 (46 percent). Edwards was mostly clean, 10 of 14 for 120 yards with no interceptions, but he lost the ball on the play that cost UNC the most, a second-quarter blitz by Koen Entringer that produced a strip sack and a Louisville recovery at the UNC 21. Seven points followed within two minutes. With 9:20 left in the third quarter, T.J. Capers sacked him again, and he left with a neck sprain and concussion symptoms.

Burgess, a true freshman, replaced him in his first college appearance, and his line tells two stories. He completed 6 of 11 for 59 yards, ran eight times for 35, and led a 9-play, 75-yard touchdown drive that pulled UNC within two. He also threw the interception that ended the game and had a fourth-down incompletion early in the fourth quarter, knocked down at the line by Jerod Smith, that turned the ball over on downs at the Louisville 21 with UNC down 13-7. Louisville then drove 60 yards for the field goal that made it 16-7. Be fair to him on the last one: the ball bounced off Shipp's hands before Mack caught it. The earlier one is a decision worth questioning. UNC went for it on fourth-and-4 from the Louisville 21, which would have been a 38-yard field goal, after Verhoff had missed from 39 earlier in the half. I think the miss shaped the call, but that's my inference. Nobody said it.

Running back

Benjamin Hall led the room with 38 snaps (64 percent) and scored both touchdowns, one from 11 yards on the opening drive and one from 6 yards with 3:43 left, on 10 carries for 48 yards. Demon June took 18 snaps (31 percent) and ran seven times for 44 yards, a 6.3 average. Jaylen McGill, the listed starter, played five snaps (8 percent), which confirms Hall as the lead back and McGill as a role player now. Hall and June averaged 5.4 yards a carry together on 17 carries. The ground game wasn't the problem. The official 114 net rushing yards on 33 attempts understates it, because the NCAA counts the 38 yards lost on six sacks against the rushing total.

Offensive line

The continuity was total, and the results weren't. Jordan Hall, Christo Kelly, Andrew Threatt and Jacqawn McRoy played all 59 snaps. Cristian Alvarez played 56, and Aidan Banfield, who has been out of the starting group, took the other 3. UNC allowed six sacks and Louisville finished with seven tackles for loss for 36 yards in the official stats. When five men play nearly every snap and the line still gives up a sack on one in five dropbacks, the problem is protection, not rotation. I don't have individual pass-blocking grades, so I can't say whose sacks they were. I flagged this matchup as a top concern before the game, and it went the way I feared.

Tight end

Jelani Thurman played every snap (59), went from questionable on Wednesday to probable on Friday, and caught 5 passes for 72 yards, a 14.4 average. He didn't score, and my preview had him scoring. UNC also leaned on a second tight end more than the depth chart suggested. Carson Sneed took 15 snaps, Shamar Easter 3 and Jordan Washington 2, so tight ends other than Thurman logged 20 snaps, roughly a third of the offense. The depth chart going in had Washington second and Sneed fourth, and Sneed played more than seven times as many snaps as Washington. Whatever the intent was, in this game Sneed was the second tight end.

Wide receiver

Jordan Shipp played 56 snaps (95 percent) and caught 7 passes for 61 yards. Trech Kekahuna played 39 (66 percent), Nyqir Helton 30 (51 percent), Mason Humphrey 27 (46 percent) and Jaden Jefferson 3. With Kymistrii Young and Keeyun Chapman out, UNC ran a four-man rotation behind Shipp, and production dropped sharply after him. UNC completed 16 passes for 179 yards. Shipp and Thurman accounted for 12 of the catches and 133 of the yards, so every other receiver and every back combined for four catches and 46 yards. Helton, the listed starter, played about half the snaps and Humphrey, who isn't on the chart, played nearly as many. UNC needs a second option, because a room like this lets a defense focus on Thurman and Shipp.

04

Defense by position group

The defense did enough to win. Louisville gained 302 yards at 4.7 a play, converted 5 of 13 third downs, punted five times and scored 16 points, which is 27.5 fewer than it had averaged over its previous four games. The Cardinals ran 33 times for 148 yards (4.5 a carry) and Lincoln Kienholz went 23 of 31 for 154 yards, 5.0 per attempt, with one touchdown and no interceptions. Their longest pass was 26 yards. Louisville ran eight red-zone plays, seven of them on the ground, and came away with one touchdown, the 3-yarder set up by the fumble, and three field goals. Hilbert hit from 46, 32 and 36 and missed a fourth wide right just before halftime. The defense also created no takeaways. The official stats credit UNC with one sack, one quarterback hit and two tackles for loss, no interceptions and no forced fumbles. Holding Louisville down wasn't enough without a takeaway. Patterson and Smith played every defensive snap, so I'm using 68 as the base for the shares below.

Defensive line

Melkart Abou Jaoude played 67 snaps (99 percent) and Jaylen Harvey 56 (82 percent), the two edges. Xavier Lewis played 43 (63 percent), and the interior pair of Leroy Jackson and Isaiah Johnson split the work evenly at 38 snaps each (56 percent). Donovan Hoilette added 13, Nicco Maggio 9 and Trey Giddens 1. Five of the eight men who played took 38 or fewer snaps, so UNC rotated its interior heavily and the front still didn't produce.

The pre-game call was that Louisville had the better line, and the result supports it more than UNC's defensive numbers suggest. Louisville sacked UNC six times. UNC hit Kienholz once and sacked him once, and he completed 74 percent of his throws. Lewis, who came in with seven sacks, was a rotation piece at 63 percent. The one sack came from a safety, Greg Smith, not from the front. Against the run the line did its job: 4.5 a carry for an offense that came in ranked among the top ten in rushing. Keyjuan Brown still ran 11 times for 78 yards, including 49 on the drive that ended in the field goal that made it 16-7, and Isaac Brown added 12 carries for 59, so the two of them combined for 23 carries and 137 yards.

Two names from the injury report don't appear in the snap counts. Jimmy-Phrisco Alo-Suliafu, listed probable on Friday, and Emmanuel Nwaiwu, listed questionable, didn't play. Maggio and Giddens took 10 snaps between them.

Linebacker

Derek McDonald played 65 snaps (96 percent), Peyton Seelmann 45 (66 percent) and Timir Hickman-Collins 26 (38 percent). The two-linebacker front means 136 linebacker snaps split three ways, and Hickman-Collins took 26 of them. Seelmann, who had 4.5 sacks coming in, was on the field for two-thirds of the game, so Hickman-Collins was a real part of the rotation rather than a depth name.

The linebackers were the group the preview gave to UNC, and the outcome backed it. Louisville's quarterback threw for 154 yards, its longest completion was 26 yards, and its tight end, Skinner, had four catches for 33 yards and the one touchdown, the short one after the fumble. Beyond Smith, I don't have individual tackle numbers for UNC defenders, so I can only rank the linebackers by usage, not production.

Defensive back

Jaiden Patterson and Greg Smith played all 68 snaps, Kaleb Cost 67 (99 percent) and Jalon Thompson 56 (82 percent). Smith was the most productive defender I have a line for, with a sack and two tackles for loss. The safety rotation stood out. Coleman Bryson played 37 snaps (54 percent), Javion Butts 31 (46 percent) and Peyton Waters 11 (16 percent), who returned after missing UConn with a shoulder injury and was listed probable. On the depth chart going in, Butts was the starting free safety. He played 31 snaps to Bryson's 37, so either the plan changed during the week or the depth chart doesn't match how Belichick wants to use the position. I don't know which. Kenton Dopson III, who was behind Thompson at corner, didn't appear.

The secondary held up about as well as the preview's very slight edge suggested. Louisville completed 74 percent of its passes but averaged only 5.0 yards on them, and Lawayne McCoy, who returned from injury, led the Cardinals with 6 catches for 64 yards. The group also had no interceptions on 31 attempts. The coverage didn't lose the game, and it didn't win it either.

Kicking and punting

Verhoff missed the 39-yard field goal in the third quarter, UNC's only attempt, and made both extra points. UNC punted four times for a 36.8 average, with none inside the 20. Louisville's Niall Hayes punted five times for 185 yards with a 45-yarder and two inside the 20, and Hilbert was 3 of 4. Jacob Horvath, who held for Verhoff against UConn, was out, and I don't know who held on Saturday.

The rotations that did move had real decisions behind them. The quarterback split was forced, 32 snaps to 27, and it was the biggest change in the game because it put a first-time college player in a one-score game. The safety group changed shape from the depth chart, with Bryson at 37 snaps, Butts at 31 and Waters at 11. Sneed, not Washington, was the second tight end by a margin of 15 snaps to 2. Humphrey played 27 snaps, nearly half of Shipp's total, at a position where the depth chart lists only three receivers. At running back, McGill's five snaps and Hall's 38 say the carry split is settled, and Hall's two touchdowns were the offense's only scores. Banfield's three snaps behind Alvarez are the only line change, and I wouldn't read much into them.

Two players on the injury report didn't play. Alo-Suliafu and Nwaiwu aren't in the counts, and neither is Dopson, who had been behind Thompson at corner. The two big questions coming in, Thurman and Waters, both played, and both were used differently. Thurman was on the field for every snap and finished with a team-best 72 receiving yards. Waters got 11 snaps and was the seventh man in the secondary rotation. I think UNC trusted Thurman's health completely and eased Waters back in, a distinction the identical probable designation didn't make.

The quarterback split matters most. UNC's two quarterbacks together dropped back 31 times and were sacked six. At least two of those came against Edwards, the Entringer strip sack and the Capers sack that ended his day, and at least one came against Burgess, Clark's sack on the final drive. I can't assign the other three. If most of them came after the change, the loss is partly a story about a freshman behind a line that had been protecting someone else. If most came before, the line was the story all along.

What the preview got right and wrong

The position-group calls held up well and the score I put on top of them didn't.

Seven of the eight edges I gave before the game landed the way the game went, and I still picked the wrong team by writing a 34-31 shootout that none of those calls supported.

The call that missed was the special teams and intangibles edge I gave UNC, and it mattered because a missed field goal, two failed fourth downs and a turnover shaped the game. The preview also noted Louisville's season turnover margin of plus 7 against UNC's minus 1, and the game went 2-0 to the Cardinals. The fumble produced the only touchdown their offense scored.

The score is where I was wrong. I gave Louisville the better quarterback, the better offensive line, the better defensive line and the better run game, and I still had UNC scoring 34 points. Those calls couldn't produce that number, and the group calls were the part that was right. I think I leaned on the posted total of around 60 and on both teams' recent scoring to set the tone of the game, and that set it too high. The final total was 30. I also had Thurman scoring a ninth touchdown to put UNC ahead late. He caught five passes for 72 yards, played every snap and never reached the end zone. The point about him was right, because he was the best receiver on the field. The touchdown was a guess.

I couldn't have called Edwards leaving the game, and I won't blame the preview for it. What I can fairly criticize is that I named UNC's pass protection as a concern and then wrote a score that assumed it would hold. It decided the game, and the score should have reflected that.

Where UNC goes from here

UNC is 5-5 and 2-4 in the ACC with two games left. One win makes the Tar Heels bowl-eligible. Fourteen points was a season low for a team that had scored at least 30 in each of its previous three games, and it came in a game where the defense held Louisville to 16.

The first question is Edwards. He left with a neck sprain and concussion symptoms, which puts his availability for Virginia in doubt. If he can't play, Burgess starts at Virginia after a debut that showed both a 75-yard touchdown drive and the final interception, and UNC would need its protection to be better than six sacks on 31 dropbacks allows. Next is the receiver room, where Shipp and Thurman produced 133 of the 179 passing yards and everyone else produced 46. Then comes the safety plan, since Butts played fewer snaps than Bryson and Waters is still coming back. Last is fourth-down decision-making, since UNC went 0 for 2 and the second failure ended the game. Verhoff's miss is part of that conversation, and so is the question of who holds for him with Horvath out.

The brawl is the loose end. Players from both teams were still shoving after Louisville took its final knee, and the flashpoint was Xavier Lewis and Louisville lineman Johnnie Brown III. Bill Belichick helped pull his players away and said afterward that you play the game between the whistles. No ejections were reported, and it's unclear whether either school or the ACC will discipline anyone. If the league does act, Lewis played 43 snaps, so it's worth watching this week.

Louisville, at 7-3 and 6-2, hosts Pittsburgh on Saturday still in the conference race.
